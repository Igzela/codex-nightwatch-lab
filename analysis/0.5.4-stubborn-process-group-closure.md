## 1. Executive Summary

Nightwatch 0.5.4 delivers the final stubborn-descendant process-group containment closure for Google Antigravity (AGY) provider turns. In Nightwatch 0.5.3, provider turns were isolated in dedicated process groups (`start_new_session=True`), but escalation returned early if the leader process exited (`process.poll() is not None`), allowing stubborn child processes ignoring `SIGINT` to survive and continue mutating the workspace.

Nightwatch 0.5.4 replaces leader-driven termination with Linux process-group liveness authority:
1. **Process-Group Liveness Authority**: Group liveness (`os.killpg(pgid, 0)`) strictly drives the abort escalation sequence (`SIGINT` -> `SIGTERM` -> `SIGKILL`). Early return occurs only when the entire process group is empty, regardless of whether the leader exited.
2. **Leader Death Escalation Continuation**: When an AGY leader exits on `SIGINT` while child descendants ignore `SIGINT`/`SIGTERM`, escalation continues to `SIGKILL` to reliably terminate the entire process tree.
3. **Safe Process-Group Identity**: Guarded with `pgid > 1`, `pgid != os.getpgrp()`, and `pgid == process.pid`. The supervisor's own process group is never signaled.
4. **Manual Stop Full Escalation**: `Supervisor.request_stop()` sets `_stop_event`, and `run_turn()` detects the event and invokes `_abort_process_group()`, ensuring bounded full escalation even when both leader and descendants ignore signals.
5. **Deterministic Verification & Edge Cases**: 15 new unit and integration tests prove signal ordering (`SIGINT` -> `SIGTERM` -> `SIGKILL`), absence of `SIGKILL` on graceful exit, unrelated process survival, and group-liveness boundary edge cases.
6. **Real Local Acceptance**: Both foreground and live user-systemd runs passed with clean verification and `DONE` status.
